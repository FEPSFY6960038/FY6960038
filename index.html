Introduction

.

Cars have changed since Karl Benz created the first in 1886 (Longdon, 2024, History of Cars Timeline: The Evolution of the Automobile). Since then, they have continued to evolve to the point where Tesla Roadster may bring us closer to flying cars (Car and Driver, 2026, 2029 Tesla Roadster). When we think of cars, we often think of speed; fast cars are massively entertaining, proven by movie franchises (e.g. Fast and Furious) and motorsports competition such as Formula 1. Formula 1 cars may be getting slower (Lathia, 2026, F1 Cars Are 3% Slower in 2026. Last Time It Took Three Years to Recover), but interest has increased (Formula 1, 2026, Formula 1 2026 Season half year review) and the speeds of up to 378 kmph (Red Bull, 2026, How fast can F1 cars go?) are part of the reason why. But how do they achieve such high speeds?

Aerodynamics

.

There are three main parts of achieving the speed of Formula 1 cars, one of which is aerodynamics. Aerodynamics is “the study of forces and the resulting motion of objects through the air” (NASA, 2023, Guide to Aerodynamics). Aerodynamics is necessary to deal with the drag force generated by air (NASA, 2023, Guide to Aerodynamics) which “opposes the [car’s] motion through the air” and thus reduces the car’s maximum speed (The STEM Spectrum, 2024, Aerodynamics Behind Formula One). The solution: to give the cars a sleek, smooth body, so that the air flows around the car reducing the drag the car experiences (The STEM Spectrum, 2024, Aerodynamics Behind Formula One). Another force a car will have to deal with is lift (The STEM Spectrum, 2024, Aerodynamics Behind Formula One), so it is necessary to generate “vertical aerodynamic force” to give the car better grip but also allow it to go faster around corner (Formula 1, 2024, F1 Explains: Downforce and Why F1 Cars Have Wings – With McLaren Aero Specialist Emel Cankaya). This downforce is generated by the wings, which work like upside down airplane wings (Formula 1, 2024, F1 Explains: Downforce and Why F1 Cars Have Wings – With McLaren Aero Specialist Emel Cankaya). These have the bonus of giving the drivers better grip on the road and more control (The STEM Spectrum, 2024, Aerodynamics Behind Formula One)
